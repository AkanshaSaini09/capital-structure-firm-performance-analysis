## 1. Project Overview

Capital structure refers to the combination of debt and equity that a company uses to finance its operations, investments, and growth. Choosing the right mix of financing is an important corporate finance decision because it influences profitability, financial risk, borrowing capacity, and shareholder value.

This project examines the relationship between capital structure and firm performance by comparing two industries with different business characteristics: the automobile industry and the Information Technology (IT) industry.

The automobile industry is capital-intensive and requires substantial investment in manufacturing plants, machinery, inventory, and distribution networks. IT companies generally follow a more asset-light business model, relying on human capital, technology, and intellectual property.

These differences influence how companies finance their operations and manage financial risk.

The study compares capital structure patterns and financial performance using key financial ratios, including the Debt-to-Equity Ratio, Interest Coverage Ratio, Return on Assets (ROA), and Return on Equity (ROE). It also discusses the implications of under-leveraging and over-leveraging and provides industry-specific recommendations.

## 2. Objectives of the Study

## 3. Industry Selection

### 3.1 Automobile Industry

The automobile industry requires significant investment in factories, machinery, production equipment, inventory, research and development, and distribution networks.

Its financial characteristics include:

- High fixed-asset requirements.
- Significant working-capital needs.
- Exposure to economic cycles and consumer demand.
- Potentially substantial capital expenditure.
- Financial pressure when sales decline while fixed costs and debt obligations remain.

Debt can help automobile companies finance expansion and benefit from the tax deductibility of interest. However, excessive borrowing can increase financial distress risk, particularly during periods of weak demand or lower operating margins.

### 3.2 Information Technology Industry

The IT industry generally follows a more asset-light business model. Its major resources include skilled employees, software, intellectual property, digital infrastructure, and client relationships.

Its financial characteristics include:

- Relatively lower physical asset requirements.
- Potentially strong internal cash generation.
- Lower dependence on debt financing in many established companies.
- Greater flexibility in allocating capital.
- Opportunities to invest in technology, research, talent, and acquisitions.

Lower debt can reduce financial risk, although excessive conservatism may limit the potential benefits of using debt strategically.

### 3.3 Why Compare These Industries?

Comparing these sectors helps demonstrate that capital structure decisions depend on the nature of a company's operations. A financing strategy suitable for an asset-light IT company may not be appropriate for a capital-intensive automobile manufacturer.

The comparison therefore focuses on the balance between financing costs, profitability, financial flexibility, and risk.
